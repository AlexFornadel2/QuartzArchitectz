The Seagram Building

New York City, USA - 2015

The Seagram Building, located at 375 Park Avenue in New York City, is widely hailed as a masterpiece of the International Style and a definitive icon of high modernism. Its creation was heavily influenced by Phyllis Lambert, the daughter of Seagram CEO Samuel Bronfman, who pushed for a design of lasting architectural merit. The building represents the quintessential expression of Mies’ "less is more" philosophy, prioritizing structural clarity and material purity over traditional applied ornamentation.

The tower’s facade is celebrated for its unprecedented use of approximately 1,500 tons of bronze, which provides a rich visual texture and depth. To satisfy fire codes that required structural steel to be encased in concrete, Mies used non-structural bronze I-beams as vertical mullions to express the building's internal framework externally. Every detail was meticulously controlled to maintain a uniform appearance; for instance, the window blinds were designed to operate in only three positions—fully open, halfway, or closed—to prevent a disorganized look from the street.

Beyond its physical form, the Seagram Building revolutionized urban planning through its pink granite public plaza. By setting the tower back 90 feet from Park Avenue, the architects created a generous open space that effectively invited public interaction in a dense metropolitan area. Today, the building remains a standard against which modern skyscrapers are judged, embodying a perfect union of corporate prestige and civic order.
